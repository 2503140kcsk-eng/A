# Long-term research roadmap

## Goal

Characterize the Pareto-minimal stationary/dynamic regret profiles attainable by one nonanticipating policy in the oblivious stochastic bandit model when cumulative variance envelope Q and mean-variation budget B are unknown.

A complete result may be positive or negative. Do not force an algorithmic success claim if a lower bound rules it out.

## Milestone 2 — formalize the stochastic model

Represent finite arm and round types; reward distributions supported on [0,1]; policy as a predictable action kernel on histories; filtration generated by actually observed actions/rewards; per-round variance envelope, mean variation, and dynamic pseudo-regret.

Use Mathlib measure-theoretic probability and martingale libraries. Do not introduce stronger independence or measurability assumptions just to simplify formalization; list any necessary strengthening explicitly.

Acceptance: definitions and basic lemmas compile, and the relationship between formal definitions and paper definitions is documented.

## Milestone 3 — reproduce the known one-change frontier

- Translate the deterministic one-improvement family into an executable finite model.
- Reproduce baseline/change coupling and expected-count argument.
- Derive both sides of Phi_g(r) frontier in the paper's exact parameter regime.
- Cross-check small finite cases by exact rational enumeration/linear programming.

Acceptance: machine-checkable finite model plus independently reviewed mathematical proof. Computational enumeration is a sanity check, not a proof of the asymptotic theorem.

## Milestone 4 — unrestricted noisy lower-bound search

- Extend hidden-improvement mixture so reward variance is positive and variance envelope is controlled.
- Audit and, if valid, formalize the candidate Bernoulli/KL lower bound in [NOISY_LOWER_BOUND_CANDIDATE.md](NOISY_LOWER_BOUND_CANDIDATE.md).
- Identify which observations carry information about (a) an improvement and (b) the noise level.
- Derive change-of-measure constraints with exact dependence on A,T,Q,B.
- Search for two or more environment families whose simultaneous requirements force a nontrivial adaptation penalty.
- Separate an information-theoretic lower bound from an artifact of a chosen algorithm.

Acceptance: theorem with fully specified environment class, lower-bound proof, and no environment chosen after observing the learner's private randomness.

## Milestone 5 — prior-free upper-bound design

Candidate directions to investigate, not assumed correct:
- geometric grids over candidate restart horizons and clipping widths;
- master/corralling constructions over supplied-budget base learners;
- online variance estimation with predictable confidence sequences;
- independent probes for detecting unobserved improvements;
- explicit budget for the meta-algorithm's own exploration cost.

For every candidate, account for the fact that only the selected arm's reward is observed. Full-information expert aggregation is insufficient unless its feedback estimator and variance cost are proved.

Acceptance: explicit policy using only allowed inputs and observations, plus expected or high-probability bound uniform over all admissible Q,B.

## Milestone 6 — match the frontier

Compare best upper bound against the lower bound from Milestone 4. If a gap remains, identify its parameter regime and whether it is logarithmic, polynomial, or structural.

Acceptance: matching rates up to clearly stated factors, or a refined impossibility theorem explaining why a proposed target is unattainable.

## Milestone 7 — external verification and publication readiness

- Recheck closest work with version/date records.
- Ask specialists to review model conventions and lower-bound coupling.
- Rerun Lean and finite computational checks from a clean checkout.
- Publish a self-contained paper draft with theorem assumptions, limitations, and complete formal sources.
- Do not submit a claimed open-problem solution until novelty and correctness have been independently reviewed.

## Stop conditions

Revise the target if a prior paper proves the exact profile under the same feedback and budget conventions; the adaptation objective is internally inconsistent; an upper-bound argument uses unobserved reward information; a formal proof requires an unproved oracle, axiom, sorry, or hidden assumption; or the model changes without every theorem and comparison being updated.
